Neuron morphology source (3D anatomy)
=====================================

  b36380a36d2a6dda0f03c946c433524b25ea2268  (2023-03-30)
  https://github.com/openworm/CElegansNeuroML
  path: CElegans/generatedNeuroML2/<NAME>.cell.nml

302 files committed verbatim, one per neuron in data/names.json. The upstream
directory holds 303 .cell.nml files; the extra one is MDL08, a body-wall
muscle, and is not copied here. Coverage of names.json is exact — every one of
the 302 neurons has a traced morphology, nothing is interpolated or invented.

Provenance of the geometry itself: the 3D model was produced by Dr. Christian
Grove and Dr. Paul Sternberg at the VirtualWorm project (WormBase, CalTech)
and released into the PUBLIC DOMAIN, digitised from the White, Southgate,
Thomson & Brenner (1986) serial-section electron micrographs. OpenWorm
converted the WormBase Virtual Worm Blender files into these NeuroML2
multi-compartmental cells. So the coordinates below are a tracing of a real
animal, not a layout algorithm.

Known properties of these exact files, verified by direct inspection here:

  - Units are micrometres. Axes in the native frame are NOT the conventional
    ones: y is anterior-posterior (nose negative, tail positive, range
    -349.5 to 448.4), z is dorsoventral, x is left-right. Checked against
    known anatomy rather than assumed — IL1DL (nose) sits at y = -282.8 and
    PLML (tail) at y = +410.1. wormed/pipeline/morphology.py remaps these
    into the renderer's frame and asserts the orientation.

  - A <segment> carries a <distal> point always and a <proximal> point only
    sometimes. When <proximal> is absent the segment starts at its PARENT's
    distal point — that is the NeuroML rule, and ignoring it disconnects the
    arbor into a cloud of floating sticks instead of a neuron. Segment ids
    are sparse and NOT in parent-before-child order, so the parent's distal
    must be resolved from a full id map, not from the preceding element.

  - Segment id 0 is the soma in every one of these files, with proximal ==
    distal (a sphere, not a cylinder). That point is what data/positions.json
    now records as the cell-body coordinate.

  - 13,869 segments across the 302 cells.

What these files do NOT contain: synapses. The .cell.nml files are pure
morphology. Connectivity comes from NeuronConnect.csv in the parent directory
(Varshney et al. 2011), and the two sources are joined only by neuron name.
Upstream's own README is explicit that its synapse and ion-channel modelling
is placeholder work; none of that is used here, only the traced geometry.
