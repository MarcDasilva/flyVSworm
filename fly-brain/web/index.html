Circuit

Outer ring: EPG heading neurons, placed by wedge (the numbers; several per wedge in the hemibrain circuit). PEN_L sit a little counterclockwise of their wedge and excite EPG counterclockwise of it; PEN_R the reverse. D7 in the centre inhibit EPG (in the hemibrain circuit mostly on the far side of the ring). A synapse lights up when a spike crosses it. Weights are fixed: nothing here learns or grows new synapses. How the fly trades

  1. Price → drive. The price's momentum over the last ~40 bars becomes a signal. Inside a dead band nothing happens. Past it, PEN_L gets +d and PEN_R gets −d (push-pull), between 0.6× and 1.3× the neurons' threshold current, changing by at most 0.3× per bar. No drive for the first 20 bars while averages fill.
  2. The bump turns. Rising price turns the heading bump counterclockwise (up the wedge numbers), falling price clockwise. With no drive it holds still: that is the circuit's memory.
  3. Turning → trade. The bump's turning speed over the last 500 ticks sets the position: counterclockwise = long, clockwise = short, full size at 7 wedges/s, scaled by bump strength. No single bump → flat.

The market is synthetic. A momentum rule profits on synthetic trends by construction, so the P&L shows the mechanism working, not trading skill.
